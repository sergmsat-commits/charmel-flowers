A fresh bouquet can last a week or even longer in a vase — as long as you take care of a few simple things from day one. Here are the rules that really work.

## Before you put the flowers in a vase

- **Wash the vase.** Hot water with washing-up liquid is best. Bacteria left on the walls clog the stems and shorten the life of the flowers.
- **Fill it with cool or room-temperature water.** If your bouquet comes with a sachet of flower food, dissolve it as described on the packet.
- **Trim the stems.** Cut 1–2 cm off each stem at an angle with a sharp knife or secateurs — this gives the flower a larger surface to drink through. Put the flowers in water straight after cutting.
- **Remove the lower leaves.** Anything that would sit below the waterline should come off — leaves rot quickly in water.

## Daily care

- **Change the water every 1–2 days**, and straight away if it turns cloudy. Rinse the vase while you're at it.
- **Trim the stems** by about 1 cm each time you change the water.
- **Top up the water.** Some flowers, such as hydrangeas and tulips, drink a lot — the level can drop within a single day.
- **Remove faded flowers and yellowing leaves** so they don't spoil the water and the rest of the bouquet.

## Where to put your bouquet

- **Away from direct sun, radiators and draughts.** Heat and dry air make flowers wilt much faster.
- **Not next to fruit.** Ripening fruit (apples and bananas, for example) releases ethylene — a gas that speeds up wilting.
- **Somewhere cool at night.** If you can, move the bouquet to a cooler room overnight — it noticeably extends its freshness.

## Tips for specific flowers

**Hydrangeas** drink not only through the stem but also through their petals. If the head wilts, submerge the whole flower in cool water for 30–60 minutes — it usually recovers quickly. On normal days, lightly misting the petals helps.

**Roses** sometimes "bend their necks" — the head droops because air got into the stem. Cut a few centimetres off the stem, ideally under water, and stand the rose in a deep container of cool water for a few hours.

**Sunflowers** have heavy heads and drink a lot — use a tall, stable vase with plenty of water and strip most of the leaves from the stems.

**Dahlias** are beautiful but delicate: they like cool water, ideally changed every day.

## Wreaths and dried flowers

Dried-flower arrangements don't need water — quite the opposite. Keep them away from moisture and strong sunlight, which fades the colours faster. Dust is easiest to remove with a hair dryer on the lowest power and cold air.

## A few myths

Sugar, aspirin or a coin in the vase are popular home remedies, but none of them replaces a clean vase, fresh water and regular stem trimming. If you have flower food, use it; if not, simply change the water more often.
